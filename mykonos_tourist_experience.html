About Mykonos

Situated at the heart of the Cyclades archipelago in the Aegean Sea, Mykonos is world-renowned for its pristine cubic architecture, golden sand beaches, and energetic coastal lifestyle.

Spanning approximately 85.5 square kilometers, the island is characterized by brilliant sunlit landscapes, traditional whitewashed buildings with vibrant Aegean blue doors, wind-swept hills, and narrow maze-like footpaths originally designed to confuse raiding pirates.

Whether seeking tranquil Mediterranean beach relaxation, ancient archaeological day trips, or world-famous dining, Mykonos offers an unmatched blend of authentic historic culture and modern luxury.

Chronicles of the Island

History of Mykonos

Mythology

The Battle of Hercules & Giants

According to Greek mythology, Mykonos was formed from the petrified bodies of giant titans defeated by Hercules and Zeus. The island was named after Mykons, a descendant of Apollo.

Antiquity

Gateway to Sacred Delos

In antiquity, Mykonos played a vital supporting role as a trade and supply hub for neighboring sacred Delos, believed to be the mythical birthplace of Apollo and Artemis.

13th - 18th C.

Venetian Rule & Flour Mills

Under Venetian governance, the famous "Little Venice" waterfront was built. During this era, wind-powered flour mills (Kato Mili) were constructed to harvest strong meltemi winds.

20th C. - Today

Modern Luxury Tourism Era

Starting in the 1950s, international celebrities and artists discovered Mykonos, turning it into one of the world's premier luxury Mediterranean destinations.

Top Attractions & Activities

Landmark

The Kato Mili Windmills

Iconic 16th-century whitewashed windmills standing on a hill overlooking Chora, built by Venetians to grind wheat.

Landmark

Little Venice (Alefkandra)

Charming 18th-century fishing houses built directly over the water with vibrant wooden balconies.

Culture

Panagia Paraportiani Church

An architectural masterpiece consisting of five interconnected whitewashed churches built over centuries.

Beach

Psarou & Paradise Beaches

World-famous golden beaches offering crystalline waters, luxury lounges, water sports, and beachside music.

Culture

Delos Island Day Trip

A 30-minute boat ride leads to the UNESCO World Heritage archaeological site of Apollo's birthplace.

Landmark

Strolling Matogianni Street

Cobblestone alley draped in bougainvillea, filled with luxury boutiques, cozy cafes, and local artisanal shops.

Travel Tips & Interesting Facts

Visitor Recommendations

Local Food & Gastronomy

Be sure to taste Kopanisti (spicy artisanal Mykonian cheese), fresh grilled octopus, and Amygdalota (almond sweets).

Best Time to Visit & Transport

May to June and September to October offer mild temperatures with fewer crowds. Renting an ATV or using KTEL buses is ideal for exploring.

Sunset Hotspots

Arrive at Little Venice or Armenistis Lighthouse at least 45 minutes before sunset for unhindered Mediterranean horizon views.

Expandable Trivia & Fun Facts

Who was Peter the Pelican?

Rescued by a local fisherman in 1958, Peter the Pelican became the official mascot of Mykonos for over 30 years. Today, successor pelicans roam Chora's waterfront freely!

Why are buildings whitewashed with blue trim?

Originally, whitewash lime acted as a thermal reflector and natural disinfectant against summer heat. In 1955, blue and white colors were officially codified to preserve Aegean heritage.

Are there over 365 chapels on the island?

Yes! Mykonos boasts more than 365 whitewashed chapels—one for each day of the year. Historically, families built private family chapels to pray for sailors' safe returns.
